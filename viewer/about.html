The book and the file

Sanzo Wada (和田三造, 1883–1967) was a Japanese painter, art teacher, and designer of costume, kimono, and fashion. In 1927 he founded the Japan Standard Color Association, now the Japan Color Research Institute. He received the 1954 Academy Award for Best Costume Design for Gate of Hell, and in 1958 was named a Person of Cultural Merit. That biography is the one Seigensha prints with the book.

His color study Haishoku Soukan (配色総鑑, 1933–34) is the source of these plates. Seigensha republished it in July 2010 as the bilingual pocket book A Dictionary of Color Combinations (配色事典, ISBN 978-4-86152-247-5). On the page for the later Volume 2, the publisher says Die Zeit called the 2010 book a source of inspiration full of “hues we didn’t even know existed,” and describes the series as a reference for designers, fashion and interior specialists, illustrators, photographers, and writers. This site is Volume 1 only.

The screen colors are white-balanced scans of that edition, checked against the printed English names. They are close to the page and still a photograph of ink. Deep Indigo, Black, and Dull Violet Black are easy to separate in the book and hard to separate on a display. For print, start from the CMYK numbers in the data, not from a conversion of the hex.

Who made this

Bill Mageni compiled and maintains the dataset. The code is MIT. The compilation is CC BY 4.0. Please credit him, and credit Sanzo Wada when you show the combinations as Wada’s. The book, its cover, and its prose remain with Seigensha and its licensors.

Which color belongs to which combination was also checked against Matt DesLauriers’ public dataset. The two agree on all 1,032 slots. The screen hexes and the CMYK correction for Etruscan Red are this project’s own reading.
